What is implemented

A PyTorch CNN feature extractor feeds an eight-feature projection into an eight-qubit variational quantum layer (PennyLane, analytic default.qubit simulator, backpropagation). The classical comparator keeps the original 128-unit dense head. This is a CNN with a variational quantum layer, not a full quantum convolution/pooling network.

Why the hybrid scores sit near 0.5

The diagnostic CLI reads the trained hybrid checkpoint and measures the signal at the quantum layer for all 24 fixtures. Encoded angles reach about 1 radian, but the eight Pauli-Z expectation values vary across images by a standard deviation of only 0.0144 on a −1 to +1 scale, and the Linear(8,1) weights average 0.1092 in magnitude. The classifier therefore receives nearly the same input for every image, so every score lies between 0.5056 and 0.5154. This is an observation about this short synthetic run, not a general property of quantum layers.

Reproducibility check

The September 2026 runs were repeated with the November 2026 code. Every metric, confusion matrix and selected epoch is identical; losses and scores differ by at most 6.0e-08 (float32 rounding). Elapsed time changed from 2.402 s / 1.542 s to 1.307 s / 1.013 s (classical / hybrid), so runtime is not a benchmark. Automated tests: 39 passed.
